What to write in an apology that actually lands

By Aniket Gurav, founder of Little Gasp, Mumbai · Updated 3 November 2026

A sorry that doesn't land usually fails in the same few ways: it's vague, it explains too much, or it asks to be forgiven too soon. Here's a simple way to write one that's honest and easy to accept.

Step by step

  1. Name exactly what you did"I'm sorry I cancelled on Saturday at the last minute" works. "Sorry for everything" sounds like you don't know what went wrong.
  2. Say how it felt for them"You'd planned the whole evening and I made you feel like it didn't matter." This shows you understand, which is most of what they're waiting for.
  3. Leave out the explanation, for nowReasons can wait for a calmer conversation. In the first message, an explanation reads like an excuse.
  4. Don't add a "but""I'm sorry, but you also…" cancels the sorry. If something needs talking about, talk about it later, separately.
  5. Promise something small and real"Next time I'll tell you a day before, not an hour before" is believable. "It will never happen again" usually isn't.
  6. Let them take their timeDon't ask "are we okay?" in the same message. Give them a way to answer when they're ready.

Lines you can adapt

  • "I'm sorry I snapped at you in front of your friends. You didn't deserve that."
  • "I forgot something that mattered to you, and I'm sorry."
  • "I should have listened instead of trying to fix it."
  • "I'll call when I'm running late, every time."

Questions people ask

How do I apologise to my girlfriend or boyfriend after a fight?

Wait until you're both calmer, then name what you did, say how it made them feel, skip the excuses and promise one small specific change. Give them time to answer instead of asking to be forgiven straight away.

Should I explain why I did it?

Not in the first apology. Explanations sound like excuses at that point. Once they feel heard, a calm conversation about why is much easier.
